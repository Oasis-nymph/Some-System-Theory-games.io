The SFI Double Auction — Rust, Miller & Palmer

What is a Synchronized Double Auction?

In a continuous double auction (CDA), buyers and sellers submit bids and asks in real time, and a trade executes whenever a bid crosses an ask. The SFI tournament used a synchronized variant: each round alternates between a Bid Step (all buyers submit sealed bids simultaneously) and an Ask Step (all sellers submit sealed asks, after seeing the current bids). This gives sellers a slight information advantage, mirroring real-world market microstructure.

The Tournament (1990-1991)

John Rust, John H. Miller, and Richard G. Palmer organized a competition at the Santa Fe Institute where 30 computer trading programs — ranging from simple heuristics to sophisticated AI — competed as buyers and sellers. Each trader knew only its own private value (buyer's redemption value or seller's unit cost). The key finding: simple strategies consistently matched or outperformed complex ones, and the double auction institution itself drove ~95-100% allocative efficiency.

Why the Double Auction Works

The double auction is remarkably efficient because its institutional rules — not trader intelligence — enforce competitive discipline. Even zero-intelligence traders constrained only by a budget constraint (ZI-C, Gode & Sunder 1993) achieve near-perfect efficiency. The bid-then-ask sequence, combined with competition among traders on each side, drives transaction prices toward the competitive equilibrium where supply meets demand.

Strategies in this Simulation

Fundamental — Estimates equilibrium price P* from recent transactions and bids with a margin around that estimate.

Bayesian — Maintains a normal-distribution belief over P*, updated each round via Kalman-like Bayesian updating from observed prices.

ProfitMax — Models the distribution of competing bids/asks and numerically searches for the bid/ask that maximizes expected profit.

Kaplan Sniper — The tournament-winning strategy. Waits passively until the bid-ask spread narrows below ~15%, then steals the deal with a slightly better price.

ZI-C — Zero-Intelligence Constrained. Submits random bids/asks within budget (never loses money). No learning, no strategy, no memory.

Key Results

1. Near-100% efficiency emerges even with decentralized, incomplete information.

2. Simplicity beats complexity: the Kaplan sniper (a ~20-line heuristic) outperformed Bayesian learners, genetic algorithms, and explicit optimizers.

3. Institution over intelligence: the double auction rules themselves — not trader sophistication — are the primary driver of market efficiency.
